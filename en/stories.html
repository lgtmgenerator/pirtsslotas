  • HISTORY OF SAUNA BESOMS

    A communal use of sauna besoms in sauna has been popular across Northern Europe since ancient times. A common origin for the tradition is supported by the fact that sauna in Finnish, Latvian and Lithuanian is called "pirts/pirtis".



  • HISTORY OF SAUNA BESOMS

    Skillfully built stone furnaces from 6-7th Century found during archeological excavations leadto believe that saunas have existed well before any written records. The first written record of a sauna in the territory is found in the chronical of Livonia in 12th Century..



  • HISTORY OF SAUNA BESOMS

    A sauna attendant first soaked a birch besom in a pot of hot water, then flapping the back and front of the person laying on the sweating shelf - this process being referred to as flapping.



  • THE HEALING POWER

    Historically, any treatment procedures in saunas were performed by female sauna attendants (sometimes referred to as masseueses). During these procedures attendants made sure that the sauna room is sufficiently hot as well as flapped and rubbed the person to be treated.



  • LOVE MAGIC

  • BESOM LEAVES

    A saying exists that if one finds a besom leave stuck to one's skin after having returned from sauna, it must be eaten to retain one's good health.



  • SO THE LOVER DOES NOT LEAVE...

  • COLLECTING BESOM MATERIALS

    Materials for besoms must be collected at a correct time, which depends on the weather, the latency of spring, weather the trees have blossomed etc. In most cases, material collection commences right before Summer equinox and just right after. Oak is an exception to the rule, as the materials for oak besoms may be collected until the very end of August.



  • BESOM DRYING

    After being made, besoms must be stored in a dark and dry place for a period of 1 - 2 weeks. If the room is not dry enough, the besoms shall begin to grow moldy on the inside.
